# Flight Guide — User Profiles

**Track:** LLM & AI Agent · **Demo case:** King Fahd International Airport (Dammam) · **Languages:** Arabic and English

Each profile lists who the user is, what they need, and what the agent must consider when advising them.

---

## 1. Ahmed — 16, flying alone for the first time

**Who:** Travelling from Dammam to Cairo to visit grandparents. First flight without family. Reads Arabic comfortably, basic English. Knows what a passport is; has never been through security alone.

**What he needs:** To be walked through each step without being frightened, and to know what to do if something changes (a gate change, a delay) with no adult beside him.

**What the agent must consider:**
- **Reassure rather than scare.** He is alone and this is his first time; calm him at each step.
- **Short, simple sentences. One instruction at a time.** Do not give him the whole route at once.
- **The mood stays light.** A single friendly remark is fine, but never joke about missing the flight or getting lost.
- **Language:** answer in Arabic.
- Tell him exactly who to approach if he is stuck.

---

## 2. Fatima — 41, first international flight

**Who:** Travelling from Dammam to Manila for work. Has flown domestically twice. Reads Arabic; does not read English. A confident, capable adult — but the airport signage is foreign to her.

**What she needs:** Complete, concrete detail for each step, especially at passport control and customs. She is most worried about ending up at the back of a long queue and missing her flight.

**What the agent must consider:**
- **Do not raise her fears.** Never bring up the possibility of missing the flight or of something going wrong.
- **Give every necessary detail.** She needs to know what happens at each desk and which queue is hers.
- **No small talk, no trivia.** She is an adult; speak to her as one.
- **Language:** answer in Arabic.
- **Boundary:** do not give visa, passport, or immigration advice. Explain the procedure, not the rules that apply to her case.

---

## 3. Yusuf — 29, tight connection

**Who:** Travelling Dammam → Dubai → onward, with 70 minutes between flights at Dubai. Travels monthly and knows airports well. Reads English.

**What he needs:** The fastest possible route to his gate, and a clear answer on whether he has time to do anything else.

**What the agent must consider:**
- **Speed over reassurance.** No encouragement, no explaining what security is. He knows.
- **Identify the boarding gate precisely**, then work out whether he has time for the lounge — and say so plainly.
- **Give time estimates**, not adjectives.
- **Escalation rule:** if time is short, tell him to stop following the route and go straight to an airport staff member.
- **Language:** answer in English.

---

## How the agent adapts

| | Ahmed (16) | Fatima (41) | Yusuf (29) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tone** | warm, reassuring | efficient, respectful | terse, direct |
| **Detail level** | one step at a time | complete for each desk | minimum necessary |
| **Main concern** | anxiety, being alone | queues, missing the flight | time, the connection |
| **Language** | Arabic | Arabic | English |
| **Time pressure** | low | medium | high |

The same situation produces three different answers. That difference is the evidence that the agent adapts to its user rather than giving one generic route.
